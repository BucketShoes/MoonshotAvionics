1 · Speed and Mach

Supersonic only briefly; the baro speed is unusable until about t+4 s

Smoothed with a local quadratic fit over 0.5 s and 1 s windows. The baro gives impossible speeds (−600 to +500 m/s) through 801 s. After that it looks smooth but stays well below the reconstruction until about 808 s, because the port's suction error shrinks as the rocket slows, so the indicated height rises more slowly than the real one.

Mach lines use 343 m/s. The reconstruction starts at 797.7 s; before that the accelerometer was pinned at +16 g.

2 · Baro error

Insane, then smooth but wrong, insane again, then smooth and converging

Baro height minus reconstructed height. The first disturbance (797.6–798.8 s) sits where the reconstruction passes Mach 1 on the way down. The second (799.7–800.6 s) is at about 270–290 m/s, Mach 0.8–0.85. That is where a local supersonic bubble forms over the nose shoulder, which is where the static ports sit. Between them (799.0–799.6 s) the baro is smooth for half a second, while reading about 600 m high. After 800.6 s the error decays smoothly with speed. So "baro smooth" did not mean "baro right" here, but "baro insane" did reliably mean "near Mach".

The 0–100 m range near apogee is also uncertain by tens of metres, because the reconstruction assumes vertical flight and a constant accelerometer offset (0.037 g from the pad).

3 · Baro-implied acceleration and jerk

Even smoothed, the baro implies hundreds of g during the transonic region

The second derivative of smoothed baro height against the IMU's vertical acceleration (axial specific force minus 1 g). With 1 s smoothing, the baro and IMU disagree by more than 10 g from 797.45 to 801.45 s and never again; in descent the baro-implied acceleration stays under 10 g. Inside that window the baro implies −340 to +40 g. So "disagrees with the IMU by more than 10 g", or with no IMU "more than 15 g at all", flags both transonic events and nothing else in this flight (apart from the liftoff instant).

Axes are clipped; values beyond the edge run off the chart. Jerk is the 0.2 s difference of the 1 s-smoothed acceleration.

4 · Apogee

About two end-over-end turns at apogee

Roll is the X axis (X read +1 g on the pad, and gyro X was pinned at its ±2000 °/s limit through boost, the spin). The chart shows pitch and yaw combined (the Y and Z gyro axes), which doesn't depend on roll angle. These are single instantaneous readings logged once a second, so 507 °/s is simply what the 814.4 s sample caught.

A smooth pulse fitted through the samples peaks at about 600 °/s at 814.06 s, within 0.05 s of the raw-baro apex. Every smooth pulse (σ 0.40–0.61 s) that fits within 40 °/s peaks between 550 and 800 °/s, centred 813.98–814.13 s. The area under the pulse is about 680–740° whichever fit you take, so the airframe swept roughly two full turns end over end in about 2 s around apogee, then settled to under 80 °/s and fell nose-first (X reads −1 g in descent).

The rotation was free tumbling, not the nose following the velocity vector. A nose tracking the velocity vector turns at most 68 °/s at 813.4 s and 49 °/s at 814.4 s, given the apex time; the measured rates are 5–10 times that. The airframe carried angular momentum into apogee, where the fins had no airspeed to damp it.

One oddity: at 9–10 rad/s, an IMU 10 cm from the rotation centre would feel about 1 g of centripetal acceleration. The accelerometer read under 0.1 g at that time. Either the IMU sat within 1–2 cm of the centre of mass, or the gyro readings around apogee are suspect. Worth checking where the board sits.

Dots are the logged samples. The band spans every smooth pulse shape that fits the 812.4–815.4 s samples within 40 °/s. If you assume a pulse of the best-fit width sampled at a random phase, 507 °/s means the true peak is 507–770 °/s, depending on how far the sample landed from the peak; the neighbouring samples put it about 0.34 s off, about 600 °/s.

5 · Main fallback limits

Main fallback rules against four descent cases

The rules: from 3 s after apogee (igniter done), fire the main if speed exceeds 40 m/s; if a shock was seen, allow up to 50 m/s until 10 s, then 40. Any reading over 1.7 g blocks the fallback for 1 s. A "less than 0.4 g at 8 s" drag test is also included. Separation and opening shocks in the model are illustrative spikes. Times are seconds after apogee; speeds are vertical.

Under these rules the ballistic case is caught by the speed cap at about 4.4 s (measured flight: 5.0 s), long before the 8 s drag test matters. A rocket that has popped open but has no chute fires at 10 s, when the cap drops to 40 m/s. A streamer or an inflated drogue never trips it, and the main deploys normally at altitude. The 0.4 g at 8 s test only becomes the deciding rule if the speed caps are raised.

What the barometer said, and what actually happened

Reconstruction of the last V1 flight. An AeroTech H135W-14A on a 432 g airframe. The motor never burned its delay charge, no chute came out, and it came in nose-down at about 100 m/s. On the way up the barometer was reading over a kilometre high, so the whole ascent had to be rebuilt from the accelerometer, the apogee, and the published thrust curve.

Speed

Thrust exceeds drag until T+1.6 s, so peak speed comes about half a second before burnout, not at burnout. The rocket is above Mach 1 for roughly 0.8 s. On the way down it settles onto a 100 m/s terminal velocity by about T+30 s and holds it to the ground.

Why the barometer lied — static port error against Mach

Each point is the logged altitude minus the reconstructed altitude, expressed as a pressure coefficient (the fraction of dynamic pressure the static port mis-senses). Away from the transonic region it sits at a steady 0.10–0.15, which is an ordinary value for a rocket static port. Approaching Mach 1 it roughly doubles, then collapses by a factor of five once supersonic as the shock passes over the port. That reversal is the signature — a random sensor glitch would not be centred on Mach 1.

How the reconstruction is anchored

  • Apogee2019 m AGL is measured, not modelled. Airspeed error goes to zero when the rocket stops, so the barometer is trustworthy exactly there. Checked independently: a ballistic fall from 2019 m at 100 m/s terminal takes 26.4 s. The log says 26.36 s.
  • CoastFrom about T+3.3 s the accelerometer is out of saturation and out of the launch vibration. Back-integrating it from apogee gives velocity and altitude with no drag model at all — 285 m/s at 661 m AGL at T+3.05 s.
  • BoostRebuilt from the published H135W curve (229 Ns over 2.072 s) and the 432 g / 350 g masses, with drag area fitted to that anchor. The fitted value, 4.54×10-4 m², matches the 4.6×10-4 m² derived independently from the coast deceleration. Impulse lands within ±20% of nominal; burn duration is not recoverable, because the accelerometer is railed or aliased from ignition to T+2.9 s.
  • DragSubsonic drag area is measured twice and differs: 4.5×10-4 m² climbing, 6.4×10-4 m² descending (Cd 0.50 and 0.70 on the 34 mm body). The ratio is mass- and model-independent. Assuming no asymmetry forces apogee 1.6 s too early, so the difference is required by the data, not fitted to it. The gyro says why: 3–50 °/s of pitch/yaw on the way up, 100–340 °/s coming down.
  • HeadingGPS puts the landing 686 m from the pad over a 44 s flight — about 16 m/s of mean horizontal drift. At 379 m/s that is roughly 2° off vertical, so the ascent can be treated as straight up.
